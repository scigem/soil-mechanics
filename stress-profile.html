Stress Profile

The vertical stresses in the ground, down through three layers of soil. The total stress σ is the weight of everything above a point: any surcharge q, any ponded water, and each layer's unit weight times its thickness, with γbulk above the water table and γsat below it. The pore pressure is hydrostatic from the water table, u = γw(z − zw), and negative (suction) through the capillary fringe above it. The effective stress, σ′ = σ − u, is what the grains carry: only a change in σ′ makes the soil settle or swell.

What you are seeing

  • The soil column (left). Each layer in its own brown, labelled with its unit weights. Soil below the water table is tinted blue, the capillary fringe a paler blue, and ponded water above the ground is solid blue. The dashed dark blue line (▽ WT) is the water table; the grey dotted line is the probe depth.
  • The stress plot (right). Total stress σ in black, pore pressure u in blue, effective stress σ′ in thick orange. The grey dashed line is σ′ for the frozen reference. The dots mark the three values at the probe depth.
  • At the probe depth. The three stresses at the probe, and how far σ′ has moved from the reference: shown orange when it has risen (the soil will settle) and green when it has fallen (the soil will swell). The note below explains what the change means.
