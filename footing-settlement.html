Why no E and no ν? Boussinesq's vertical stress contains no elastic constant, and that is not a coincidence: σz under a surface load is fixed by statics and by the geometry of load spreading. Reading the classical radial solution as a fan of force chains carrying cos θ reproduces σzz and σrz exactly for every Poisson ratio. The only components that need elasticity are the ones a settlement calculation never uses.

Where φ comes in. That ray fan has q/p = 1/3, so it is admissible whenever sin φ ≥ 1/3, i.e. φ ≥ 19.5°. Boussinesq is the unaligned-fabric limit. Align the force chains with the load and the friction cone binds: the fan narrows, and fitting Fröhlich's σz = (nP/2πR²)cosnθ returns a concentration factor that depends on φ alone. n = 3 at the crossover, rising to about 7 at φ = 40°. The n = 4–6 that practice recommends corresponds to φ = 25.7° to 35.4°.

What this means for the answer. Focusing the load does not change the total force on any horizontal plane — it moves it inward. So the centre settles more than Boussinesq predicts, the neighbour settles less, and the bowl dishes harder. Boussinesq is not the conservative choice in either direction.

What is approximated. Each vertical is compressed one-dimensionally with no shear coupling to its neighbours, which is the standard sublayer summation. This is a perfectly flexible footing; a rigid one settles uniformly and redistributes its contact pressure instead. The two bracket the real case.
